# COMPUTATIONAL BIOLOGY SKILL: Protein structure prediction & structural analysis
# Use for predicting or analyzing 3D protein structure — AlphaFold/ESMFold/Boltz, PDB/mmCIF
# handling, structure comparison, and prep for docking/MD. (For sequence-level work see
# compbio/sequence_analysis; for small-molecule/docking see drug_design/small_molecule_design.)
# Goal: a structure with its confidence honestly assessed and correctly handled downstream.

1. PICK THE PREDICTOR BY NEED:
- Fast single-sequence prediction (no MSA) -> ESMFold. Good for quick monomers.
- High accuracy / need an MSA -> AlphaFold2 (ColabFold is the practical local route; MSA quality drives accuracy). Complexes, protein-ligand, protein-nucleic-acid -> AlphaFold3 / Boltz-style models. Strong homolog with a known structure -> template/homology modeling is cheaper and often enough.

2. INPUTS & PROVENANCE:
- Use the EXACT sequence you mean — strip or deliberately keep expression tags (His-tag), signal peptides, and cleaved regions; they change the fold. Record the tool + version + the MSA database/date. A poor or empty MSA yields a poor AlphaFold model — garbage in, garbage out.

3. TRUST THE CONFIDENCE METRICS — DO NOT EYEBALL THE CARTOON:
- pLDDT (per-residue, 0-100): >90 very high, 70-90 confident backbone, 50-70 low, <50 usually means INTRINSIC DISORDER / flexibility — NOT necessarily a wrong fold. Don't discard low-pLDDT loops as "errors"; they're often genuinely flexible.
- PAE (Predicted Aligned Error, a 2D matrix): read it for MULTI-DOMAIN and COMPLEX reliability — it tells you how confident the RELATIVE position of two domains/chains is. A confident-looking single-domain fold can still have unreliable inter-domain or inter-chain placement. For complexes, PAE is the metric that matters.

4. HANDLE PDB/mmCIF CORRECTLY:
- Parse with Biopython `Bio.PDB` (`PDBParser` / `MMCIFParser`), or PyMOL/RDKit as appropriate. Prefer mmCIF for large structures — the legacy PDB format has fixed column widths, an atom/chain-count ceiling, and can't represent huge assemblies. Decide deliberately whether to keep waters, ions, and hetero-atoms.

5. DOWNSTREAM PREP & COMPARISON:
- Before docking or MD: add hydrogens, assign protonation states at the target pH, and fix missing atoms/loops (a raw predicted or crystal structure is not simulation-ready).
- Comparing structures: superpose and report RMSD for same-length/aligned cases, but use TM-score for structures of DIFFERENT lengths (TM-score is length-normalized; raw RMSD is misleading across different sizes). TM-score >0.5 ≈ same fold.

6. VALIDATE & DOCUMENT:
- Sanity-check geometry (clashes, Ramachandran outliers) and biological plausibility, not just "it produced a PDB." Save the input sequence, tool/version/DB, the confidence metrics (pLDDT/PAE summary), and your interpretation of which regions are trustworthy.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Reading pLDDT<50 as "the prediction is wrong" when it usually means disorder/flexibility.
- Ignoring PAE and trusting a complex/multi-domain model's relative geometry.
- Feeding a bad/empty MSA (or the wrong sequence with tags) and trusting the output.
- Using the fixed-width PDB format for very large structures (use mmCIF).
- Docking/simulating a raw structure without protonating and fixing missing atoms.
- Comparing different-length structures by RMSD instead of TM-score; treating one predicted model as "the" definitive structure.
