# RESEARCH SKILL: Multi-source evidence synthesis
# Use when answering a consequential or broad question requires current evidence from several sources, comparison, and explicit uncertainty.

## When to use

Use for literature or landscape reviews, option comparisons, due diligence, and
questions whose answer depends on reconciling multiple current sources. Skip it for
a simple factual lookup, casual brainstorming, or analysis centered on local source
code rather than external evidence.

## Preconditions

- Define the question, decision or deliverable, relevant timeframe, and evidence
  threshold. Make a reasonable scoped assumption unless a missing choice would
  materially change the result.
- Identify which claims require current external verification and what source types
  can support them.

## Procedure

1. Break the question into the smallest useful set of distinct evidence questions;
   do not manufacture dimensions merely to make the work look comprehensive.
2. Search directly and favor current primary or authoritative sources. Use secondary
   sources for context or competing interpretations, and track dates and scope.
3. Cross-check material claims. Record agreements, contradictions, missing evidence,
   and whether a conclusion is reported fact, source interpretation, or inference.
4. Use read-only sub-agents only for large, genuinely independent evidence threads.
   Collect every spawned report and verify consequential claims against its sources.
5. Synthesize around the user's actual question, decision criteria, and tradeoffs,
   not around search order or delegation structure.

## Verification

- Place source links near the claims they support and ensure each source actually
  supports the stated scope.
- Distinguish current facts, dated facts, inference, and unresolved uncertainty.
- Check important numbers, identities, versions, and dates against primary evidence
  when available.

## Stop or adapt

If sources invalidate the premise, reframe the question explicitly. If evidence is
conflicting or incomplete, report the disagreement rather than forcing consensus.
Stop when the defined evidence threshold is met or further searching has diminishing
value; a finite unsuccessful search is not proof that something does not exist.
